## Scope and version

This lesson connects the supplied security, privacy, unit-cost, and environmental concepts. It reflects the listed standards and guidance available on 2026-11-20; operational details vary by provider and jurisdiction. Authorization model names are not mutually exclusive products. Privacy-enhancing methods reduce particular risks but do not remove the need to define the data, threat model, and privacy guarantee. Carbon and cost measurements require consistent boundaries and workload units.

## How this group fits the mental model

For every request, determine **who or what is calling**, **what action is requested**, **which resource and tenant are involved**, and **what evidence authorizes that action**. Then protect the path and credentials, make deployed software traceable, and measure what each unit of service costs and consumes. Security and sustainability are properties of the whole workload: a perfect access model does not fix a leaked token; a low PUE does not make an inefficient workload efficient; an inexpensive cloud bill does not prove that each customer receives value.

## Supplied names explained

- **Authentication** — Establishes an identity or verifies a claimed identity, such as a user login or workload credential. It answers “who/what is the caller?” Authentication alone does not decide what the caller may do.
- **Authorization** — Evaluates whether an authenticated subject may perform an action on a resource under policy. Check it at a trusted server boundary and for the particular object and operation; UI visibility and a valid login are not authorization.
- **Role-based access control (RBAC)** — Grants permissions through assigned roles. Roles are useful when job functions map to stable permission bundles. Poorly designed systems accumulate many overlapping roles or overly broad permissions; role membership changes and privileged operations need review and audit.
- **Attribute-based access control (ABAC)** — Evaluates attributes of subject, resource, action, and sometimes environment against policy. It can express contextual rules such as “a clinician may read an assigned patient record during an approved shift.” Attribute quality, provenance, freshness, policy conflicts, explainability, and test coverage become essential.
- **Relationship-based access control (ReBAC)** — Computes access from relationships among users, groups, and objects, often represented as tuples or a graph: a user is a member of a team; the team is a viewer of a document. It fits collaborative products and nested sharing. Relationship changes, inherited access, consistency, and large-graph query latency require explicit design. Google Zanzibar is a published example of a consistent global authorization system, not a universal ReBAC product standard.
- **Mutual TLS (mTLS)** — TLS in which both peers present and validate certificates. It can authenticate a client/workload and encrypt the connection. OAuth certificate-bound tokens can make a stolen bearer token less useful without the matching private key. mTLS does not itself authorize a user to read a particular document, rotate certificates, or secure traffic that bypasses the mTLS endpoint.
- **Secrets manager** — A service and API for controlled storage, access, versioning, and often rotation of credentials or other secrets. Grant runtime identities only the needed secret/version; keep secrets out of source, logs, client bundles, and broad environment dumps. Rotation is a workflow: change the upstream credential, publish the new version, move consumers safely, verify, then retire the old credential. Caches, staged rollouts, and rollback affect the actual exposure window.
- **Web application firewall (WAF)** — An HTTP-layer control that inspects requests and applies managed or custom rules to block or rate-limit common web attack patterns. It complements secure application code, identity checks, API authorization, input validation, and DDoS controls; it cannot reliably understand every business rule or prove a request is safe. Rules need logging, tuning, false-positive handling, and bypass testing.
- **Data residency and erasure** — Residency states where data and relevant copies are permitted to be stored or processed; erasure is the governed handling of eligible deletion requests, subject to the law and any applicable exceptions. Inventory primary stores, replicas, logs, caches, derived indexes, backups, processors, and key copies. Do not promise universal instantaneous erasure when immutable backup retention or legal holds apply; define “beyond use,” expiry, restore reconciliation, and evidence with counsel and the data-protection owner.
- **Differential privacy** — A mathematical framework that bounds how much the output of an analysis can change when one individual's record is included or removed, under a stated neighboring-dataset definition and privacy parameters. The privacy budget composes across repeated releases; smaller epsilon generally means stronger protection but can reduce utility. Correctness depends on contribution bounds, noise, accounting, implementation, and who controls the data. “Anonymized” is not an adequate substitute for a documented guarantee.
- **Federated learning** — Training in which participating clients or organizations compute local updates and a coordinator aggregates updates instead of centralizing raw training examples. This can reduce direct data movement and enable collaboration, but gradients or model updates may reveal information, participants may be malicious, and data quality/participation may be uneven. Federated learning by itself is not differential privacy or secure aggregation; combine controls such as secure aggregation, clipping, differential privacy, robust validation, and governance when the threat model requires them.
- **Software supply-chain provenance** — Verifiable evidence describing how an artifact was built, including its source and build process, tied to the artifact digest. SLSA defines levels/tracks and provenance attestation formats; a signed attestation is useful only if the builder identity, signing trust, policy, and artifact binding are verified. Provenance complements—not replaces—SBOMs, vulnerability response, reproducible or isolated builds, and artifact access control.
- **Carbon-aware scheduling** — Shifts flexible compute in time or location using grid-carbon signals while honoring deadlines, data residency, latency, cost, and reliability requirements. It is most suitable for deferrable batch or training jobs. Forecast uncertainty, grid-region boundaries, transmission, water, hardware lifecycle, and rebound effects matter; scheduling to a “green” region is not automatically lower-impact for every system boundary.
- **Power usage effectiveness (PUE)** — Data-center total facility energy divided by IT equipment energy over a stated measurement period and boundary. Lower PUE means less facility overhead relative to IT energy, but PUE does not measure useful work per unit energy, carbon intensity, water use, or whether the IT workload itself is efficient. Compare like-for-like boundaries and periods; cloud customers often cannot measure a provider's facility PUE for a single workload.
- **FinOps unit economics** — Relates technology cost and usage to a meaningful unit of resource efficiency or business output, such as cost per API call, tenant, successful transaction, GB processed, or case resolved. Choose the unit based on a real decision and consistently allocate shared costs. Pair cost with outcome and quality; a lower cost per transaction is not an improvement if success rate or latency degrades.
- **Energy-per-workload measurement** — Estimates or measures energy consumed for a defined amount of useful work, such as watt-hours per 1,000 successful image transformations or energy per completed training run. State whether the boundary is server IT power, whole facility, client/device, networking, or a provider estimate. Include failed/retried work where observable. This measure complements PUE and carbon emissions; energy and emissions differ because grid carbon intensity varies over time and location.

## Cross-item comparison

| Need | Candidate | Critical limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Stable permission bundles for organization jobs | RBAC | Role explosion or excessive privileges if roles are too broad |
| Context rules from subject/resource/action attributes | ABAC | Depends on trustworthy, current attributes and comprehensible policy evaluation |
| Sharing and inheritance through object relationships | ReBAC | Relationship consistency, revocation, and graph query scale matter |
| Authenticate service-to-service connections | mTLS / workload identity | Authentication at transport does not replace application authorization |
| Protect credentials throughout their lifecycle | Secrets manager | Rotation consumers, caches, logs, and rollback must all cooperate |
| Reduce common HTTP attack traffic | WAF | Rules can miss business-logic abuse and create false positives |
| Publish aggregate statistics with a formal privacy bound | Differential privacy | Privacy budget and utility must be designed across all releases |
| Train across data owners without central raw data collection | Federated learning | Updates can leak; it is not a privacy proof by itself |
| Verify how a deployed artifact was produced | Provenance attestation | Verification policy and trusted builder identity are required |
| Reduce grid emissions for flexible work | Carbon-aware scheduling | Must respect deadline, residency, reliability, and true impact boundary |
| Compare facility overhead | PUE | Does not express application energy efficiency or carbon intensity |
| Compare cloud cost to value | Unit economics | Allocation and output quality must be comparable |

## Examples

**Tenant document sharing:** a user authenticates with OIDC, and the API authorizes a `read` action by checking a relationship tuple for the requested document. The policy service receives the tenant and object from trusted server state, not a client-supplied owner field. A short cache can improve latency, but revocation and relationship-consistency requirements determine cache TTL or consistency token. A WAF may filter malformed HTTP attacks but cannot replace this object-level decision.

**Workload credential rotation:** a worker authenticates to the secrets manager with its managed workload identity, reads a narrowly scoped database credential, and caches it for a bounded period. A rotation process creates a new credential accepted alongside the old one, publishes a new secret version, rolls workers, verifies successful connections, and retires the old credential. Monitoring reports version adoption without logging the secret value. If consumers retain the old credential indefinitely, scheduled rotation alone has not reduced risk.

**Private analytics and ML:** a group wants population-level trends from data held at several institutions. Federated learning keeps raw rows local but sends model updates. Secure aggregation can restrict the coordinator's view to an aggregate, while differential privacy can bound information released about a contributor. The team defines a contribution unit, clipping, privacy accountant and epsilon/delta parameters, collusion assumptions, membership attacks, release budget, and accuracy floor before production. None of the techniques prevents an authorized consumer from inferring more than the stated model/guarantee allows.

**Cost and sustainability review:** measure cloud cost and estimated energy per 10,000 successful API operations, including retries and shared service allocation. Report PUE only if provider data uses a comparable facility boundary; report carbon separately using location/time-based grid intensity. Move a deferrable batch aggregation job to a lower-carbon window only if its deadline and data locality permit, and compare total system energy and cost before and after.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing authentication with authorization or treating OAuth as an end-user authentication protocol by itself; OIDC is the identity layer built on the OAuth framework.
- Treating RBAC, ABAC, and ReBAC as exclusive choices. They can be composed, but policy precedence and explainability need explicit rules.
- Assuming mTLS means every downstream request is authorized or that certificate provisioning and revocation happen automatically.
- Rotating a stored password without coordinating upstream validity, cached consumers, connection pools, and rollback.
- Assuming a WAF, privacy filter, or managed rule set blocks all attacks or validates business authorization.
- Claiming federated learning keeps information private merely because raw training records stay local.
- Reporting an epsilon value without the neighboring relation, composition/accounting method, population, and all releases in scope.
- Treating signed provenance as true without validating the signer, builder, artifact digest, and policy.
- Optimizing PUE, dollar cost, or emissions in isolation while useful work, reliability, water, and latency worsen.
- Applying carbon-aware scheduling to online interactive requests that cannot tolerate delay or cross-region processing.

## Knowledge checks with answers

1. **What is the key difference among RBAC, ABAC, and ReBAC?** RBAC uses assigned roles, ABAC evaluates attributes and policy, and ReBAC evaluates relationships among subjects and resources. Each can be appropriate at a different policy boundary.
2. **Does mTLS authorize an API action?** It can authenticate a peer and protect the channel; application policy must still decide whether that identity may perform the specific action on the resource.
3. **Does federated learning guarantee differential privacy?** No. It changes where training occurs and what is exchanged. Privacy guarantees need explicit mechanisms and threat analysis, such as secure aggregation and formally accounted differential privacy.
4. **What does a PUE of 1.2 tell you?** For the stated data-center boundary and period, facility energy is 1.2 times IT equipment energy. It says nothing by itself about energy per useful transaction or grid emissions.
5. **How should a team define cost per unit?** Select a measurable unit aligned with a business or engineering decision, allocate shared cost consistently, and track service quality and value alongside cost.
6. **What makes a provenance attestation useful?** It is bound to the exact artifact digest, produced by an identified builder, protected against tampering, and evaluated under a verification policy before deployment/use.
